\begin{abstract}
\textcolor{blue}{nFAPI-based Split Option 6 separates the MAC scheduler and High-PHY across packet-switched transport, where scheduling messages must arrive at the PNF within a strict timing window. Existing static slots ahead scheduling either over-provisions lead time, increasing MAC HARQ round-trip time (RTT), or under-provisions it, causing late arrivals, missing-slot events, and throughput degradation under network jitter. This paper proposes an adaptive timing control framework for nFAPI Split 6 that dynamically adjusts the scheduler trigger offset using PNF Timing Info feedback. The proposed method applies EWMA-based arrival-margin and jitter estimation to increase the slots ahead value rapidly under timing risk and decrease it conservatively under stable conditions. We implement the framework in an OpenAirInterface-based end-to-end testbed with a commercial O-RU and COTS UE. Experimental results show that the proposed method maintains stable throughput under injected delay and jitter while avoiding unnecessary MAC HARQ RTT inflation compared with static slots ahead baselines. Additional multi-switch/router deployment results further confirm that the proposed software-based method remains effective under realistic multi-hop transport conditions.}
\end{abstract}

\begin{IEEEkeywords}
nFAPI, O-RAN, Functional Split, Adaptive Control.
\end{IEEEkeywords}

\section{Introduction}
\subsection{Background and Motivation}

\textcolor{blue}{Functional split architectures have been widely studied as a key enabler for flexible and cost-efficient 5G/O-RAN deployments, where different protocol functions can be distributed across centralized and distributed nodes depending on bandwidth, latency, and coordination requirements \cite{Larsen2019}, \cite{3GPP_TR_38_801}, \cite{Alam2026}. In nFAPI-based Split Option 6, the MAC scheduler in the VNF must send DL\_TTI.request messages sufficiently ahead of the target slot so that the PNF can process them within its timing window. If the message arrives later than the deadline, the PNF discards the scheduling request, resulting in missing-slot events and throughput degradation.}

\textcolor{blue}{Existing OAI nFAPI-style implementations rely on statically configured slots ahead scheduling. Such a static configuration creates an inherent throughput-latency trade-off: a larger slots ahead value improves robustness against delay and jitter but increases MAC HARQ RTT, whereas a smaller value reduces latency but becomes vulnerable to late packet arrivals. Since HARQ retransmission timing is tightly coupled with MAC scheduling and resource availability, excessive scheduling lead time may inflate MAC-layer feedback latency and reduce the efficiency of HARQ process reuse \cite{Elfiky2024}. When functional split interfaces are carried over non-ideal packet-switched fronthaul or midhaul networks, variable queueing delay and transport jitter may violate the strict timing constraints imposed by lower-layer processing \cite{Diez2021}, \cite{Huang2017}, \cite{PerezRomero2023}.}

\textcolor{blue}{However, existing open-source implementations such as OAI have historically relied on simplified slot-indication forwarding and static slots ahead configuration, leaving the SCF-defined Timing Info based delay-management loop incomplete. The SCF nFAPI specification defines software-level Node Sync and Timing Info procedures to support delay management between VNF and PNF entities, enabling timing compensation without relying solely on hardware-triggered slot indications \cite{SCF225}, \cite{SCF222}.}

\textcolor{blue}{To resolve this trade-off, this paper proposes an adaptive timing control framework for nFAPI Split 6. Our approach dynamically adjusts the scheduler trigger offset based on closed-loop feedback using PNF Timing Info reported by the PNF. By applying an Exponentially Weighted Moving Average (EWMA) model, the proposed method estimates the real-time arrival margin and transport jitter. The proposed method then applies a fast-increase and conservative-decrease policy to dynamically update the slots ahead, securing system stability under congestion while optimizing latency under stable network conditions.}

\textcolor{blue}{This paper addresses the following problem: how to maintain nFAPI Split 6 scheduling stability over non-deterministic packet-switched transport without relying on hardware-level synchronization or static worst-case over-provisioning. The key challenge is that the scheduler must choose a slots ahead value before observing the future network delay. A conservative value improves robustness but increases MAC HARQ RTT, while an aggressive value reduces latency but risks late packet arrivals and slot loss. To resolve this trade-off, we propose a closed-loop delay-management method that uses PNF Timing Info to estimate the arrival margin and adapt the slots ahead value online.}

\subsection{Related Works}

\textcolor{blue}{Recent advancements in Open RAN (O-RAN) architectures have driven significant research into optimizing split-architecture performance and managing network latency. These works can be broadly classified into three categories.}

\textcolor{blue}{\textbf{Hardware-assisted Split 7.2:} Hardware-assisted O-RAN testbeds such as X5G \cite{Villa2025} demonstrate the feasibility of high-throughput end-to-end private 5G deployments by integrating OpenAirInterface with NVIDIA ARC, GPU acceleration, and high-performance networking support. However, such designs primarily improve performance through specialized acceleration and synchronization infrastructure, whereas this work targets a software-only timing adaptation method for nFAPI Split Option 6 over general-purpose packet-switched transport.}

\textcolor{blue}{\textbf{Intra-node Software Scheduling:} Lee \textit{et al.} \cite{Lee2022} proposed an adaptive low-latency downlink transmission method for FAPI-based 5G NR gNBs, focusing on reducing intra-node processing delay within the MAC-PHY interaction. In contrast, our method addresses cross-node timing uncertainty caused by VNF-PNF transport delay and jitter in nFAPI Split Option 6. Similarly, Aquifer \cite{Aquifer2024} optimized OS-level scheduling microservices to absorb processing delay spikes, but does not handle cross-node timing violations caused by unpredictable transport delay and jitter.}

\textcolor{blue}{\textbf{nFAPI Delay Management Gap:} nFAPI has been proposed as a standard interface for SDN-based 5G small cell networks \cite{Douros2018}, and open-source implementations of 5G NR Split Option 6 have demonstrated its feasibility in practical platforms \cite{Wang2022}. Nevertheless, existing implementations still largely rely on static trigger offsets, leaving the SCF-defined Timing Info based delay-management loop underexplored. Our proposed EWMA-based adaptive slots ahead adjustment method fills this critical gap, providing a cost-effective, software-only solution for COTS servers.}

\textcolor{blue}{Specifically, compared to Split 7.2 implementations like X5G \cite{Villa2025} that require hardware-level PTP synchronization and GPU/SmartNIC offloads, or FAPI-based Split 6 designs \cite{Lee2022} restricted to intra-node shared memory and process-level adjustments, our proposed method operates at the slot/TTI level on standard COTS servers, actively managing cross-node network jitter and delay over standard packet-switched Ethernet without hardware acceleration dependencies.}

\subsection{Research Scope and Contributions}

\textcolor{blue}{In this paper, we focus on the nFAPI-based Option 6 split as a representative case of timing-sensitive yet transport-flexible RAN disaggregation. While Option 6 offers significant advantages in terms of reduced fronthaul bandwidth and deployment flexibility, its performance is highly sensitive to non-deterministic latency introduced by transport networks and server processing.}

\textcolor{blue}{To address the limitations of static configurations under such non-deterministic transport environments, this paper introduces a dynamic timing adjustment framework that achieves closed-loop synchronization stability. The main contributions of this paper are summarized as follows:}

\begin{itemize}
\item \textcolor{blue}{\textbf{SCF-compliant nFAPI timing framework for OAI Split 6:} We redesign the OAI nFAPI timing path to support Node Sync, PNF Timing Info, and Delay Management, enabling closed-loop timing control instead of simplified static slot forwarding.}
\item \textcolor{blue}{\textbf{EWMA-based adaptive slots ahead adjustment:} We propose a lightweight closed-loop delay-management method that estimates the arrival margin and jitter from PNF Timing Info and dynamically adjusts the scheduler trigger offset to balance throughput stability and MAC HARQ RTT.}
\item \textcolor{blue}{\textbf{End-to-end validation under controlled and multi-hop transport:} We implement the proposed method on an OpenAirInterface-based E2E physical testbed with a commercial Pegatron O-RU and COTS UE. We evaluate throughput, MAC HARQ RTT, missing-slot behavior, and stability under both Linux-TC-injected delay/jitter (under Experimental Scenario I) and a realistic 3-switch/3-router campus network deployment (under Experimental Scenario II). The implementation has been successfully merged into the official upstream OAI codebase to support reproducibility and future research.}
\end{itemize}

\section{System Model}
\label{sec:system_architecture}

\textcolor{blue}{Before diving into the system details, this study introduces the adopted system framework. To integrate the Network Functional Application Platform Interface (nFAPI) with mainstream commercial equipment, the proposed system adopts a stable two-stage split architecture. The network first connects via the Split~6 nFAPI interface between the network functions, and then translates to the standard O-RAN Split~7.2x interface to connect to a commercial Pegatron O-RU. Figure~\ref{fig:System-Architecture} illustrates this proposed system architecture and its delay management mechanism.}

\subsection{End-to-End Architecture}
\label{subsec:A_end_to_end}
\textcolor{blue}{The system establishes a complete 5G end-to-end communication link, extending from the core to the user equipment:
\begin{itemize}
\item \textbf{Core Network (CN):} The central element responsible for routing, session management, and authenticating user data.
\item \textbf{O-RAN Central Unit (O-CU):} A logical node that handles higher-layer protocol stacks, such as the Radio Resource Control (RRC) and Packet Data Convergence Protocol (PDCP). It connects with distributed units via the F1 interface.
\item \textbf{O-RAN Distributed Unit (O-DU):} To optimize computational performance and deployment flexibility, the system separates the O-DU into an O-DU High and an O-DU Low. The O-DU High is executed as a Virtualized Network Function (VNF) on a virtualized machine, whereas the O-DU Low is deployed as a Physical Network Function (PNF) on dedicated hardware.
\item \textbf{Switch:} A commercial network switch is located between the VNF and PNF. This mid-layer connection utilizes the nFAPI protocol. However, traversing this packet-switched network inevitably generates non-deterministic latency.
\item \textbf{O-RAN Radio Unit (O-RU):} A specialized hardware unit responsible for low-level physical layer (Low PHY) processing, digital beamforming, and Radio Frequency (RF) transmission.
\item \textbf{Commercial Off-The-Shelf User Equipment (COTS UE):} Standard commercial smartphones or modems that act as the terminal receivers in the network.
\end{itemize}}

\subsection{O-DU High: MAC, Scheduler, and Timing Core}
\label{subsec:B_odu_high}
\textcolor{blue}{Within the O-DU High virtualized network function, the timing control is organized as a unified timing control system. The core processes handling this timing core include the Scheduler, Node sync, and Delay Management:
\begin{itemize}
\item \textbf{Scheduler:} Critical for allocating the available radio resources (such as time-frequency resource blocks) and determining the exact priority and timing of downlink/uplink data transmissions.
\item \textbf{Node sync:} Responsible for software-level clock synchronization. This procedure periodically exchanges synchronization timestamps ($t_1 \sim t_4$) with the PNF via standard \textit{DL/UL\_node\_sync} messages. It calculates the instantaneous clock offset and applies slot-level ($S_{adj}$) and microsecond-level ($\mu s_{adj}$) adjustments to align the VNF's local clock to the PNF baseline, eliminating long-term hardware clock drift.
\item \textbf{Delay Management:} Operates on top of the stabilized clock phase established by Node sync. This mechanism utilizes the PNF Timing Info ($\Delta t_{arrive}$) feedback from the PNF to dynamically estimate transport network jitter. It then actively adjusts the slots ahead ($S_{\mathrm{ahead}}$) trigger offset of the scheduler. This control compensates for the non-deterministic packet transport delays and VNF processing delays without inducing synchronization loss.
\end{itemize}}

\subsection{O-DU Low: High PHY and Timing Window}
\label{subsec:C_odu_low}
\textcolor{blue}{The O-DU Low manages the higher-level baseband processing of the physical layer (High PHY). At this level, the most critical synchronization mechanism is the nFAPI PNF Timing Window:
\begin{itemize}
\item \textbf{Timing Window:} A predefined, strict time duration. Let $T_{\mathrm{window}}$ denote the duration of this PNF timing window, and $T_{\mathrm{slot}}$ denote the subframe slot duration. When the scheduling message (specifically the \texttt{DL\_TTI.request}) sent by the O-DU High propagates across the network, it must accurately arrive and fall exactly within this Window. Otherwise, the High PHY will reject the message and fail to process the slot data.
\item \textbf{Timing Info ($\Delta t_{arrive}$):} The PNF continuously monitors the exact timing info of control messages relative to the transmission deadline. This information, denoted as $\Delta t_{arrive}$, represents the remaining time margin within the timing window. The PNF periodically sends this information back to the MAC layer for Delay Management. This establishes a closed-loop feedback control system, compensating for the unpredictable transmission latency variations between the VNF and PNF machines.
\end{itemize}}

\subsection{Timing Model}
\textcolor{blue}{Let $T_{\mathrm{slot}}$ denote the slot duration and $S_{\mathrm{ahead}}$ denote the number of slots by which the VNF scheduler triggers earlier than the target slot. For a scheduling message generated at VNF time $t_g$, the corresponding target PNF processing deadline is determined by the target slot boundary. The arrival margin reported by the PNF is denoted by $\Delta t_{\mathrm{arrive}}$, where $\Delta t_{\mathrm{arrive}} < 0$ indicates that the message arrives before the deadline, and $\Delta t_{\mathrm{arrive}} > 0$ indicates a late arrival. Therefore, the delay-management objective is to select the minimum stable $S_{\mathrm{ahead}}$ such that late arrivals are avoided ($\Delta t_{\mathrm{arrive}} \le 0$) while MAC HARQ RTT is not unnecessarily inflated.}

\subsection{Key Validation Metrics}
\label{subsec:D_metrics}
\textcolor{blue}{To evaluate the stability, synchronization accuracy, and real-time performance of the proposed algorithm under non-ideal environments, this study defines the following Key Validation Metrics:
\begin{itemize}
\item \textbf{MAC Layer HARQ RTT ($T_5 - T_1$):} Measures the Hybrid Automatic Repeat Request (HARQ) loop delay specifically at the MAC layer. This metric accurately reflects the feedback efficiency and operational health of the link layer.
\item \textbf{VNF-PNF DL Latency ($T_2 - T_1$):} Measures the one-way downlink latency accumulated from the virtualized machine (VNF) to the physical machine (PNF). This metric is used to directly quantify and analyze the delay jitter impact injected by the intermediate Ethernet switch.
\end{itemize}
Note that RTT denotes the Round-Trip Time. To validate the split-architecture stability under non-ideal fronthaul conditions, network latency simulation is conducted at the uplink ($T_5 - T_4$) and downlink ($T_2 - T_1$) segments via Linux Traffic Control (TC).}

\textcolor{blue}{To validate the proposed performance optimization within a realistic network environment, the system design leverages a commercial-grade deployment framework:
\begin{itemize}
\item \textbf{O-RAN Split 7.2x Interoperability:} While the Small Cell Forum (SCF) has standardized the nFAPI for the MAC-PHY split (Option 6) \cite{Douros2018}, commercial-grade Open Radio Units (O-RUs) natively support the O-RAN Split Option 7.2x. To achieve seamless integration with open-source stacks like OpenAirInterface \cite{Wang2022}, our system bridges these interfaces.
\item \textbf{Hybrid System Architecture for E2E Validation:} To validate the nFAPI performance over a fully commercial ecosystem, this paper adopts a hybrid architecture (Split Option 6 + Split Option 7.2). This setup establishes a translation/interworking layer that enables the nFAPI-driven MAC/High-PHY to successfully interact with a commercial Pegatron O-RU, facilitating end-to-end verification using Commercial Off-The-Shelf (COTS) UEs.
\end{itemize}}

\section{Proposed Method}
\label{sec:proposed_method}

\textcolor{blue}{This section details the proposed dynamic timing adjustment framework. To resolve the unstable latency during packet transmission between the PNF and VNF, we propose two core timing control algorithms: Node sync for software-level clock synchronization and Delay Management for slot-level adaptive delay management. The timing control of this system is organized as a closed-loop system, ensuring that the VNF-side scheduling can dynamically track and compensate for both clock drift and slot-level transport latency variation. The two algorithms cooperate as follows:}
\begin{enumerate}
    \item \textcolor{blue}{\textbf{Node sync:} Maintains phase and slot boundary alignment between the VNF and PNF. It periodically exchanges timestamps to calculate the absolute phase offset, correcting for hardware clock drift.}
    \item \textcolor{blue}{\textbf{Delay Management:} Tracks and estimates packet transport delays and jitter at the slot level, dynamically adjusting the slots ahead ($S_{\mathrm{ahead}}$) to guarantee that control messages fall within the PNF's strict timing window.}
\end{enumerate}

\subsection{Node sync}
\label{subsec:node_sync}
\textcolor{blue}{To perform software-level clock synchronization, the system adopts a timestamp exchange mechanism. The VNF periodically transmits a downlink node synchronization message with a local timestamp $t_1$. Upon receiving the message, the PNF records the local arrival timestamp $t_2$ and transmits an uplink node synchronization message back to the VNF, including the transmit timestamp $t_3$ and the echoed $t_2$. When the VNF receives the message, it records its local arrival timestamp $t_4$. Because the nFAPI timestamps are constrained by a 1024 subframe number (SFN) loop, they wrap around every $10.24$~seconds ($10240000$~\textmu s). The algorithm calculates the differences $d_1 = t_2 - t_1$ and $d_2 = t_4 - t_3$, applying a wrap-around correction to align them within the interval $[-5120000, 5120000]$~\textmu s. The VNF then calculates the instantaneous clock offset:}
\begin{equation}
\mathit{offset} = \frac{d_1 - d_2}{2}
\end{equation}
\textcolor{blue}{To prevent timing adjustments from causing control loop oscillations, the synchronization control loop implements a state-based locking mechanism. The system has two synchronization states: locked ($\mathit{sync\_locked} = 1$) and unlocked ($\mathit{sync\_locked} = 0$). In the locked state, the VNF monitors the drift of the local clock. If the absolute offset exceeds the slot duration $T_{\mathrm{slot}}$:}
\begin{equation}
|\mathit{offset}| > T_{\mathrm{slot}}
\end{equation}
\textcolor{blue}{the VNF unlocks the synchronization ($\mathit{sync\_locked} = 0$) to trigger re-synchronization. In the unlocked state, the VNF checks whether the offset has converged within the slot duration $T_{\mathrm{slot}}$. If $|\mathit{offset}| \le T_{\mathrm{slot}}$, the system locks the synchronization state ($\mathit{sync\_locked} = 1$). Otherwise, the VNF decomposes the offset into a slot-level adjustment $S_{\mathrm{adj}}$ and a microsecond-level adjustment $\mu s_{\mathrm{adj}}$ based on the slot duration $T_{\mathrm{slot}}$:}
\begin{equation}
S_{\mathrm{adj}} = \mathit{offset} / T_{\mathrm{slot}}
\end{equation}
\begin{equation}
\mu s_{\mathrm{adj}} = \mathit{offset} \pmod{T_{\mathrm{slot}}}
\end{equation}
\textcolor{blue}{These adjustments are accumulated into the global adjustment variables:}
\begin{equation}
\mathit{slot\_adjustment} \gets \mathit{slot\_adjustment} + S_{\mathrm{adj}}
\end{equation}
\begin{equation}
\mathit{us\_adjustment} \gets \mathit{us\_adjustment} - \mu s_{\mathrm{adj}}
\end{equation}
\textcolor{blue}{The negative sign in the microsecond adjustment reduces the local thread sleep time when the VNF clock is behind, speeding up the slot transition rate to catch up. The synchronization process is summarized in Algorithm~\ref{alg:node_sync_simplified}.}

\begin{algorithm}
\caption{Node sync}
\label{alg:node_sync_simplified}
\begin{algorithmic}[1]
\algrenewcommand\algorithmicrequire{\textbf{Input:}}
\algrenewcommand\algorithmicensure{\textbf{Output:}}
\Require $t_1, t_2, t_3$ (Timestamps from PNF \textit{UL\_node\_sync}), $t_4$ (VNF local receive timestamp)
\Ensure $\mathit{slot\_adjustment}$ (Slot adjustment accumulator), $\mathit{us\_adjustment}$ (Microsecond adjustment accumulator)

\Statex \textbf{Notation:} 
\Statex $\mathit{offset}$ (Instantaneous timing offset), $\mathit{sync\_locked}$ (Synchronization lock state)
\Statex $T_{\mathrm{slot}}$ (Slot duration in \textmu s), $T_{\mathrm{wrap}}$ (Wrap-around period, $10240000$ \textmu s)
\Statex $\text{WrapCorrection}(x, T)$ (Corrects $x$ within $[-T/2, T/2]$)

\Statex \textbf{Phase I: Timing Offset Calculation}
\State $d_1 \gets \text{WrapCorrection}(t_2 - t_1, T_{\mathrm{wrap}})$
\State $d_2 \gets \text{WrapCorrection}(t_4 - t_3, T_{\mathrm{wrap}})$
\State $\mathit{offset} \gets (d_1 - d_2) / 2$

\Statex \textbf{Phase II: Hysteresis Lock and Clock Adjustment}
\If{$\mathit{sync\_locked} = 1 \land |\mathit{offset}| > T_{\mathrm{slot}}$}
    \State $\mathit{sync\_locked} \gets 0$ \Comment{Unlock due to excessive clock drift}
\EndIf

\If{$\mathit{sync\_locked} = 0$}
    \If{$|\mathit{offset}| \le T_{\mathrm{slot}}$}
        \State $\mathit{sync\_locked} \gets 1$ \Comment{Lock synchronization}
    \Else
        \State $S_{\mathrm{adj}} \gets \mathit{offset} / T_{\mathrm{slot}}$ \Comment{Integer division}
        \State $\mu s_{\mathrm{adj}} \gets \mathit{offset} \pmod{T_{\mathrm{slot}}}$
        \State $\mathit{slot\_adjustment} \gets \mathit{slot\_adjustment} + S_{\mathrm{adj}}$
        \State $\mathit{us\_adjustment} \gets \mathit{us\_adjustment} - \mu s_{\mathrm{adj}}$
    \EndIf
\EndIf
\State \Return $\mathit{slot\_adjustment}, \mathit{us\_adjustment}$
\end{algorithmic}
\end{algorithm}

\subsection{Delay Management}
\label{subsec:delay_management}

\textcolor{blue}{To counteract changing network latency and maintain scheduling stability, a dynamic delay-management strategy is implemented. This strategy operates as a closed-loop feedback mechanism based on standard Small Cell Forum (SCF) PNF Timing Info reported by the PNF. By evaluating the arrival margin relative to the PNF deadline, the proposed method dynamically adjusts the scheduling lead time $S_{\mathrm{ahead}}$.}

\subsubsection{EWMA-based Arrival-Margin Estimation}
\textcolor{blue}{To smooth high-frequency network noise while remaining sensitive to timing drift, the proposed method maintains a running mean of the PNF Timing Info ($\mu_{\mathrm{arrive}}$) and the estimated jitter ($dev_{\mathrm{arrive}}$). The EWMA structure is inspired by the classical TCP round-trip time estimation method, where a smoothed estimate and a deviation estimate are jointly maintained to tolerate delay variation while remaining responsive to congestion-induced timing changes \cite{Jacobson1988}, \cite{Paxson2011}. Let $\Delta t_{\mathrm{arrive}, i}$ be the observed signed PNF Timing Info in scheduling slot $i$. The EWMA updates for mean arrival margin and jitter are defined as:}
\begin{equation}
\mu_{\mathrm{arrive}, i} = (1 - \alpha) \mu_{\mathrm{arrive}, i-1} + \alpha \Delta t_{\mathrm{arrive}, i}
\end{equation}
\begin{equation}
dev_{\mathrm{arrive}, i} = (1 - \beta) dev_{\mathrm{arrive}, i-1} + \beta |\Delta t_{\mathrm{arrive}, i} - \mu_{\mathrm{arrive}, i}|
\end{equation}
\textcolor{blue}{Following this dual-gain estimation principle, the smoothing factors are set to $\alpha = 1/8$ for long-term arrival-margin tracking and $\beta = 1/4$ for fast jitter-variation estimation. Both parameters are negative powers of two ($\alpha = 2^{-3}$, $\beta = 2^{-2}$), which allows efficient fixed-point implementation using bitwise shift operations in the timing-critical MAC path.}

\textcolor{blue}{The combined timing risk indicator $R_i$ is evaluated to determine whether the arrival margin crosses the deadline boundary:}
\begin{equation}
R_i = \mu_{\mathrm{arrive}, i} + dev_{\mathrm{arrive}, i}
\end{equation}
\textcolor{blue}{A positive timing risk ($R_i > 0$) implies that the estimated worst-case arrival margin crosses the deadline, indicating an anomaly condition, whereas a negative risk indicates a safe margin.}

\subsubsection{Adaptive Slots Ahead Control}
\label{subsubsec:delay_control}
\textcolor{blue}{The update of the slots ahead trigger timing $S_{\mathrm{ahead}}$ is governed by a fast-increase and conservative-decrease control law to optimize latency while preserving throughput under dynamic congestion:}
\begin{itemize}
    \item \textbf{Rapid Increase (Preserve Throughput):} \textcolor{blue}{If an anomaly timing risk is detected ($R_i > 0$), indicating that the scheduling message is crossing the PNF processing deadline, the proposed method immediately increases the slots ahead value to prevent late packet arrivals and missing-slot dropouts:}
          \begin{equation}
          S_{\mathrm{ahead}, i+1} = \min\left(S_{\mathrm{ahead}, i} + \left\lceil \frac{R_i}{T_{\mathrm{slot}}} \right\rceil, S_{\max}\right)
          \end{equation}
          \textcolor{blue}{and the stable observation counter $C_{\mathrm{stable}}$ is reset to 0. Here, $S_{\max} = \lfloor T_{\mathrm{window}} / T_{\mathrm{slot}} \rfloor$ represents the maximum allowed slots ahead.}
    \item \textbf{Gradual Decrease (Optimize Latency):} \textcolor{blue}{If the system is under stable and safe conditions ($R_i < -dev_{\mathrm{arrive}, i}$), indicating that there is a sufficient timing margin, the proposed method decrements the slots ahead value by one slot once the stability condition is satisfied ($C_{\mathrm{stable}} \ge C_{\mathrm{threshold}}$):}
          \begin{equation}
          S_{\mathrm{ahead}, i+1} = \max\left(S_{\mathrm{ahead}, i} - 1, S_{\min}\right)
          \end{equation}
          \textcolor{blue}{and resets $C_{\mathrm{stable}} = 0$ to prevent premature timing reductions. Here, $S_{\min} = 1$ is the minimum slots ahead trigger offset.}
    \item \textbf{Maintain Baseline:} \textcolor{blue}{Otherwise, the trigger timing remains unchanged:}
          \begin{equation}
          S_{\mathrm{ahead}, i+1} = S_{\mathrm{ahead}, i}
          \end{equation}
          \textcolor{blue}{and the stable counter $C_{\mathrm{stable}}$ is incremented if the current sample is safe ($R_i \le 0$).}
\end{itemize}

\textcolor{blue}{The core delay management strategy is condensed in Algorithm~\ref{alg:delay_management}.}

\begin{algorithm}
\caption{Delay Management}
\label{alg:delay_management}
\begin{algorithmic}[1]
\algrenewcommand\algorithmicrequire{\textbf{Input:}}
\algrenewcommand\algorithmicensure{\textbf{Output:}}
\Require $\Delta t_{\mathrm{arrive}}$ (Observed PNF Timing Info), $S_{\mathrm{curr}}$ (Current trigger timing), $T_{\mathrm{window}}$ (Timing window), $T_{\mathrm{slot}}$ (Slot duration)
\Ensure $S_{\mathrm{next}}$ (Trigger timing for the next MAC schedule)
\Statex 
\Statex \textbf{Notation:} 
\Statex $\mu_{\mathrm{arrive}}$ (smoothed mean PNF Timing Info), $dev_{\mathrm{arrive}}$ (smoothed arrival jitter)
\Statex $R$ (Timing risk indicator), $C_{\mathrm{stable}}$ (Stable observation counter)
\Statex $C_{\mathrm{threshold}}$ (Stability observation threshold), $S_{\max}, S_{\min}$ (Boundary limits)
\Statex $\alpha, \beta$ (Smoothing factors for latency and jitter)
 
\Statex \textbf{Phase I: Latency and Jitter Estimation}
\State $\mu_{\mathrm{arrive}} \gets (1 - \alpha)\mu_{\mathrm{arrive}} + \alpha \Delta t_{\mathrm{arrive}}$ \Comment{Update mean PNF Timing Info}
\State $dev_{\mathrm{arrive}} \gets (1 - \beta)dev_{\mathrm{arrive}} + \beta |\Delta t_{\mathrm{arrive}} - \mu_{\mathrm{arrive}}|$ \Comment{Update arrival jitter}
\State $R \gets \mu_{\mathrm{arrive}} + dev_{\mathrm{arrive}}$ \Comment{Evaluate combined risk indicator}
 
\Statex \textbf{Phase II: Fast-Up and Slow-Down Timing Adjustment}
\If{$R > 0$}
    \State $S_{\mathrm{next}} \gets \min(S_{\mathrm{curr}} + \lceil R / T_{\mathrm{slot}} \rceil, S_{\max})$ \Comment{Rapid Increase}
    \State $C_{\mathrm{stable}} \gets 0$
\ElsIf{$R < -dev_{\mathrm{arrive}}$}
    \If{$C_{\mathrm{stable}} \ge C_{\mathrm{threshold}}$}
        \State $S_{\mathrm{next}} \gets \max(S_{\mathrm{curr}} - 1, S_{\min})$ \Comment{Gradual Decrease}
        \State $C_{\mathrm{stable}} \gets 0$
    \Else
        \State $S_{\mathrm{next}} \gets S_{\mathrm{curr}}$
        \State $C_{\mathrm{stable}} \gets C_{\mathrm{stable}} + 1$
    \EndIf
\Else
    \State $S_{\mathrm{next}} \gets S_{\mathrm{curr}}$
    \If{$R \le 0$}
        \State $C_{\mathrm{stable}} \gets C_{\mathrm{stable}} + 1$
    \Else
        \State $C_{\mathrm{stable}} \gets 0$
    \EndIf
\EndIf
\State \Return $S_{\mathrm{next}}$
\end{algorithmic}
\end{algorithm}

\subsection{Coordination between Node sync and Delay Management}
\label{subsec:coordination}

\textcolor{blue}{To ensure stable operation under real-world conditions, Node sync and Delay Management must coordinate without inducing control loop oscillations. The microsecond-level Node sync focuses strictly on correcting clock phase offsets to align the VNF's scheduling thread with the PNF's slot transitions. Because it directly shifts the VNF's time base, any clock adjustment ($S_{adj}, \mu s_{adj}$) temporarily shifts the arrival timestamp reference. Delay Management is designed to absorb this transient shifting. By relying on history-weighted EWMA-based risk tracking ($R_i$), Delay Management avoids reacting to the temporary timing offsets caused by clock adjustments, allowing Node sync to converge. Once Node sync locks synchronization ($\mathit{sync\_locked} = 1$), the time base stabilizes, allowing Delay Management to achieve precise slot-level optimizations ($S_{\mathrm{ahead}}$).}

\section{Experimental Results}
\label{sec:experiments}

\textcolor{blue}{Experiments were conducted to validate the proposed method in an OpenAirInterface (OAI) nFAPI end-to-end environment. OpenAirInterface has been widely used as an open-source platform for 5G NR end-to-end experimentation and system-level validation \cite{Ryu2022}, \cite{Wang2022}. Each reported throughput value represents the average of 180 data points, collected from three independent trials with 60 samples per trial. The evaluation focuses on four aspects of the proposed method. First, we examine whether EWMA-based filtering can stabilize noisy PNF Timing Info feedback under transport network jitter. Second, we compare the proposed adaptive slots ahead adjustment with static baselines to evaluate its ability to maintain throughput while avoiding unnecessary MAC HARQ RTT inflation. Third, we evaluate the closed-loop delay-management method under severe congestion and dynamic jitter to verify end-to-end synchronization stability. Finally, we deploy the VNF-PNF transport path across a realistic multi-switch/router campus network to examine whether the timing-control loop remains effective in a practical multi-hop environment.}

Section~\ref{subsec:experimental_scenario} describes the hardware, software, and automated rApp evaluation framework, including both the controlled single-switch scenario and the multi-switch/router campus deployment. \section{Conclusion}
\label{sec:conclusion}

\textcolor{blue}{This paper presented a dynamic timing adjustment framework for disaggregated RAN architectures, focusing on nFAPI-based Split Option 6 over non-ideal packet-switched networks. To address the limitations of static slots ahead configurations under unpredictable network jitter and server processing delay, the proposed method applies EWMA-based arrival-margin and jitter estimation to dynamically adjust the scheduler trigger timing. Experimental validation on the OpenAirInterface platform with a commercial Pegatron O-RU shows that the proposed method reduces synchronization failures and maintains stable throughput under severe delay and jitter conditions. Compared with hardware-centric approaches that rely on specialized acceleration or precise synchronization infrastructure, the proposed software-based method provides a cost-effective and flexible alternative for robust O-RAN deployment on general-purpose COTS servers.}

\textcolor{blue}{Although the proposed software-based timing control achieves robust performance, the current evaluation focuses primarily on downlink timing control under controlled delay and jitter injection. Future work will extend the proposed method to joint uplink/downlink timing optimization, multi-cell deployments, and analytical stability bounds for the feedback loop.}
