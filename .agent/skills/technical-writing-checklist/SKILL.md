# Technical Writing Checklist

This skill provides a systematic checklist for reviewing and improving technical writing in academic papers. Use this when drafting or reviewing technical content to ensure professional quality.

## Core Principles

### 1. Clarity and Directness

**Checklist:**
- [ ] Use simple and precise words
- [ ] Avoid long, complex sentences with multiple sub-clauses
- [ ] Use Subject-Verb-Object (SVO) structure
- [ ] Ensure every sentence has **one** clear meaning
- [ ] Define all abbreviations and acronyms at first occurrence (**Define on First Use**)

**Examples:**

❌ **Avoid:** "RRM is necessary for 6G."

✅ **Use:** "Radio Resource Management (RRM) is necessary for 6G."

---

### 2. Conciseness: Cut the Clutter

**Checklist:**
- [ ] Delete unnecessary words
- [ ] Avoid "filler" phrases that do not add information
- [ ] Use short sentences for easier understanding

**Examples:**

❌ **Avoid:** "It is important to note that the system is fast."

✅ **Use:** "The system is fast."

---

### 3. Objectivity: Facts over Feelings

**Checklist:**
- [ ] Focus on data and facts
- [ ] Avoid emotional language or personal opinions
- [ ] Use a formal and neutral tone
- [ ] Support claims with quantitative evidence

**Examples:**

❌ **Avoid:** "Our novel algorithm shows big improvement."

✅ **Use:** "The proposed algorithm reduces latency by 20%."

---

### 4. Consistency: Use One Term

**Checklist:**
- [ ] Use the same terminology throughout the paper
- [ ] Do not use different words for the same concept
- [ ] Use the same text shown in figure/table captions

**Examples:**

❌ **Avoid:** Referring to your method as "the system" in one paragraph and "the framework" in the next.

✅ **Use:** Pick one term (e.g., "the framework") and use it throughout the entire paper.

---

### 5. Logical Structure

**Checklist:**
- [ ] Follow the standard IMRaD format (Introduction, Methods, Results, and Discussion)
- [ ] Start from general to specific (big to small)
- [ ] Ensure smooth transitions between paragraphs
- [ ] Ensure correlation between successive sentences and paragraphs

---

### 6. Accuracy

**Checklist:**
- [ ] Check all data, citations, and formulas
- [ ] Ensure all claims are supported by evidence
- [ ] Verify numerical results and statistics
- [ ] Cross-reference all citations

## Quick Reference Style Guide

| **Principle** | **Instead of...** | **Use...** |
|---------------|-------------------|------------|
| **Directness** | "A study was conducted by us..." | "We conducted a study..." |
| **Precision** | "The result was very big." | "The result increased by 45%." |
| **Brevity** | "... due to the fact that..." | "... because..." |
| **Objectivity** | "We believe this is a novel idea." | "This method differs from [1] in X and Y." |
| **Brevity** | "In order to provide a solution..." | "To solve..." |
| **Active Voice** | "The data was collected by us." | "We collected the data." |
| **Precision** | "The temperature was high." | "The temperature was 95°C." |

---

## How to Use This Skill

1. **During Writing:** Keep these principles in mind as you draft each section
2. **During Review:** Go through each checklist item systematically
3. **Before Submission:** Perform a final pass focusing on consistency and accuracy
4. **Peer Review:** Use this checklist to provide constructive feedback

## Common Mistakes to Avoid

- Using undefined acronyms
- Mixing terminology for the same concept
- Using vague quantifiers ("very", "big", "small") instead of precise measurements
- Including unnecessary filler phrases
- Using passive voice when active voice is clearer
- Making unsupported claims
- Inconsistent formatting or terminology
