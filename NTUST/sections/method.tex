\chapter{Proposed Method}
\label{sec:method}

This chapter details the proposed dynamic timing adjustment algorithm. To address the unstable transmission latency during packet transmission between the PNF and VNF, we propose an Adaptive Slots Ahead mechanism.

\iffalse
\section{延遲管理架構}
\fi
\section{Delay Management Architecture}
\label{subsec:timing_control_architecture}

The timing control of this system focuses on the logical layer of synchronization and delay management, ensuring that the slots ahead at the VNF side can flexibly respond to network jitter without causing unexpected oscillations. This architecture operates through two main logical components that interact seamlessly:
\begin{enumerate}
    \item \textbf{Node Sync:} Ensures alignment of frame and slot boundaries between the VNF and PNF. It monitors timing offsets to maintain synchronization, thereby absorbing low-layer transmission jitter.
    \item \textbf{Delay Management:} Focuses on slot-level delay management. It dynamically analyzes the statistical data reported by the PNF through a convergence optimization algorithm to determine the global target slots ahead ($S_{\text{ahead}}$), ensuring that scheduling commands always comply with the PNF's strict timing window constraints.
\end{enumerate}

\section{節點同步}

\section{Node Sync}
\label{subsec:node_sync}
To achieve microsecond-level phase alignment, the system adopts a timestamp exchange mechanism similar to IEEE 1588 (PTP). When the VNF receives the \textit{UL\_node\_sync} message, it calculates the instantaneous timing offset ($offset$) based on four timestamps ($t_1$ to $t_4$). To avoid instantaneous spikes, an Exponentially Weighted Moving Average (EWMA) model is used to smooth the $offset$ and its variation ($error$), generating an average offset ($\mu_{offset}$) and a mean absolute deviation ($dev_{offset}$), which are adjusted using smoothing factors $\alpha$ and $\beta$. Then, a dynamic synchronization threshold ($Threshold$) is derived directly from the estimated jitter ($dev_{offset}$). If the absolute offset exceeds this threshold, the system decomposes the offset into a slot adjustment ($S_{adj}$) and a microsecond adjustment ($\mu s_{adj}$) based on the slot duration ($\text{slot\_duration}$). These parameters are subsequently used by the VNF Timing Actuator to precisely align the local clock. This process is summarized in Algorithm~\ref{alg:node_sync_simplified}.

\begin{algorithm}[H]
\iffalse
\caption{節點同步 (Node Sync)}
\fi
\caption{Node Sync}
\label{alg:node_sync_simplified}
\small
\begin{algorithmic}[1]
\Require $t_1, t_2, t_3$ (PNF \textit{UL\_node\_sync}), $t_4$ (VNF local reception timestamp)
\Ensure $S_{adj}$ (Slot adjustment), $\mu s_{adj}$ (Microsecond adjustment)

\Statex \textbf{Notation:} 
\Statex $offset$ (Instantaneous timing offset), $\mu_{offset}$ (EWMA mean offset)
\Statex $dev_{offset}$ (EWMA mean absolute deviation / jitter)
\Statex $Threshold$ (Dynamic synchronization threshold), $\alpha, \beta$ (Smoothing factors)
\Statex $\text{slot\_duration}$ (Slot duration in \textmu s)

\Statex \textbf{Phase 1: Timestamp Processing and Wraparound Protection}
\State $d_1 \gets t_2 - t_1$
\State $d_2 \gets t_4 - t_3$

\Statex \textbf{Phase 2: Offset and Dynamic Jitter Estimation}
\State $offset \gets (d_1 - d_2) / 2$
\State $error \gets offset - \mu_{offset}$
\State $\mu_{offset} \gets \mu_{offset} + \alpha \cdot error$
\State $dev_{offset} \gets dev_{offset} + \beta(|error| - dev_{offset})$
\State $Threshold \gets dev_{offset}$

\Statex \textbf{Phase 3: Phase Alignment}
\If{$|offset| > Threshold$}
    \State $S_{adj} \gets \lfloor offset / \text{slot\_duration} \rfloor$
    \State $\mu s_{adj} \gets offset \pmod{\text{slot\_duration}}$
    \State Apply $S_{adj}$ and $\mu s_{adj}$ to adjust the local clock
\Else
    \State State $\gets$ \textbf{Synchronized}
    \State $S_{adj} \gets 0$, $\mu s_{adj} \gets 0$
\EndIf
\State \Return $S_{adj}, \mu s_{adj}$
\end{algorithmic}
\end{algorithm}

To combat changing network latency and maintain scheduling stability, this paper proposes a dynamic delay management strategy. This strategy intelligently trades off between throughput and latency by introducing the concepts of predicted risk and historical risk debt. Upon receiving the worst-case delay offset ($\Delta t_{arrive}$) reported by the PNF, the system calculates an Exponentially Weighted Moving Average (EWMA) for the mean delay ($\mu_{delay}$) and jitter deviation ($dev_{delay}$) using smoothing factors $\alpha$ and $\beta$.

Before making tracking adjustments, the system evaluates the potential tail risk ($Risk_{pred}$) based on the maximum of the observed offset and the mean delay, augmented by the jitter deviation. This risk is then accumulated into a history-weighted risk debt ($Debt_{risk}$), which represents a decaying memory of past network instability. If a deadline violation occurs ($\Delta t_{arrive} > 0$) or a high risk is predicted ($Risk_{pred} > 0$), the algorithm takes immediate action to preserve throughput by incrementing the current trigger timing ($S_{curr}$).

Conversely, to optimize latency during safe operations, the algorithm checks if the historical debt is cleared ($Debt_{risk} < \epsilon$) and if the system has maintained sustained stability over consecutive observations ($count_{stable} \ge N_{stable}$, where $N_{stable}$ scales dynamically). If both conditions are met, the algorithm cautiously decreases the advance time. In scenarios where the risk is low but stability evidence is still accumulating or debt is being cleared, the current timing is maintained. This asymmetric response ensures connection continuity while avoiding ping-pong oscillations. The core adjustment strategy is condensed in Algorithm~\ref{alg:delay_management}.

\begin{algorithm}[H]
\iffalse
\caption{延遲管理 (Delay Management)}
\fi
\caption{Delay Management}
\label{alg:delay_management}
\small
\begin{algorithmic}[1]
\Require $\Delta t_{arrive}$ (Observed timing offset), $S_{curr}$ (Current trigger timing)
\Ensure $S_{next}$ (Trigger timing for the next MAC schedule)

\Statex \textbf{Notation:} 
\Statex $\mu_{delay}$ (EWMA mean delay), $dev_{delay}$ (EWMA mean absolute deviation / Jitter)
\Statex $Risk_{pred}$ (Predicted timing risk), $Debt_{risk}$ (History-weighted risk debt)
\Statex $count_{stable}$ (Consecutive stable arrivals counter)
\Statex $N_{stable}$ (Adaptive stability threshold), $S_{max}$ (Maximum advance-time limit)
\Statex $\alpha, \beta$ (Smoothing factors, where $\alpha=1/8, \beta=1/4$)
\Statex $\epsilon$ (Debt clearance threshold)

\Statex \textbf{Phase 1: Delay, Risk and Debt Estimation}
\State $error \gets \Delta t_{arrive} - \mu_{delay}$
\State $\mu_{delay} \gets \mu_{delay} + \alpha \cdot error$
\State $dev_{delay} \gets dev_{delay} + \beta(|error| - dev_{delay})$
\State $Risk_{pred} \gets \max(\Delta t_{arrive}, \mu_{delay}) + dev_{delay}$ \Comment{Evaluate tail risk}
\State $Debt_{risk} \gets Debt_{risk} + \alpha \cdot (\max(0, Risk_{pred}) - Debt_{risk})$ \Comment{Accumulate or decay debt}
\State $IsDebtFree \gets (Debt_{risk} < \epsilon)$

\Statex \textbf{Phase 2: Trade-off Actuation (Throughput vs. Latency)}
\If{$\Delta t_{arrive} > 0 \lor Risk_{pred} > 0$} 
    \State $S_{next} \gets \min(S_{curr} + 1, S_{max})$ \Comment{Preserve throughput}
    \State $count_{stable} \gets 0$
\ElsIf{$IsDebtFree \land (count_{stable} \ge N_{stable})$} 
    \State $S_{next} \gets \max(S_{curr} - 1, 1)$ \Comment{Optimize latency}
    \State $count_{stable} \gets 0$
\Else 
    \State $S_{next} \gets S_{curr}$ \Comment{Maintain current timing}
    \If{$Risk_{pred} \le 0$}
        \State $count_{stable} \gets count_{stable} + 1$
    \EndIf
\EndIf
\State \Return $S_{next}$
\end{algorithmic}
\end{algorithm}
