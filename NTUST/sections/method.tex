\chapter{Proposed Method}
\label{sec:method}

This chapter details the proposed dynamic timing adjustment framework. To address the unstable transmission latency during packet transmission between the PNF and VNF, we propose two core timing control algorithms: Node sync for software-level clock synchronization and Delay Management for slot-level adaptive delay management. The timing control of this system is organized as a closed-loop system, ensuring that the VNF-side scheduling can dynamically track and compensate for both microsecond-level clock drift and slot-level transport latency variation. The two core algorithms cooperate as follows:

\begin{enumerate}
    \item \textbf{Node sync:} Maintains phase and slot boundary alignment between the VNF and PNF. It periodically exchanges timestamps to calculate the absolute phase offset, correcting for hardware clock drift (Clock Drift).
    \item \textbf{Delay Management:} Tracks and estimates packet transport delays and jitter at the slot level, dynamically adjusting the slots ahead ($S_{\mathrm{ahead}}$) to guarantee that control messages fall within the PNF's strict timing window.
\end{enumerate}

\section{Node sync}
\label{subsec:node_sync}
To perform software-level clock synchronization, the system adopts a timestamp exchange mechanism. The VNF periodically transmits a downlink node synchronization message with a local timestamp $t_1$. Upon receiving the message, the PNF records the local arrival timestamp $t_2$ and transmits an uplink node synchronization message back to the VNF, including the transmit timestamp $t_3$ and the echoed $t_2$. When the VNF receives the message, it records its local arrival timestamp $t_4$. Because the nFAPI timestamps are constrained by a 1024 subframe number (SFN) loop, they wrap around every $10.24$~seconds ($10240000$~\textmu s). The algorithm calculates the differences $d_1 = t_2 - t_1$ and $d_2 = t_4 - t_3$, applying a wrap-around correction to align them within the interval $[-5120000, 5120000]$~\textmu s. The VNF then calculates the instantaneous clock offset:
\begin{equation}
\mathit{offset} = \frac{d_1 - d_2}{2}
\end{equation}
To prevent timing adjustments from causing control loop oscillations, the synchronization control loop implements a state-based locking mechanism. The system has two synchronization states: locked ($\mathit{sync\_locked} = 1$) and unlocked ($\mathit{sync\_locked} = 0$). In the locked state, the VNF monitors the drift of the local clock. If the absolute offset exceeds the slot duration $T_{\mathrm{slot}}$:
\begin{equation}
|\mathit{offset}| > T_{\mathrm{slot}}
\end{equation}
the VNF unlocks the synchronization ($\mathit{sync\_locked} = 0$) to trigger re-synchronization. In the unlocked state, the VNF checks whether the offset has converged within the slot duration $T_{\mathrm{slot}}$. If $|\mathit{offset}| \le T_{\mathrm{slot}}$, the system locks the synchronization state ($\mathit{sync\_locked} = 1$). Otherwise, the VNF decomposes the offset into a slot-level adjustment $S_{\mathrm{adj}}$ and a microsecond-level adjustment $\mu s_{\mathrm{adj}}$ based on the slot duration $T_{\mathrm{slot}}$:
\begin{equation}
S_{\mathrm{adj}} = \mathit{offset} / T_{\mathrm{slot}}
\end{equation}
\begin{equation}
\mu s_{\mathrm{adj}} = \mathit{offset} \pmod{T_{\mathrm{slot}}}
\end{equation}
These adjustments are accumulated into the global adjustment variables:
\begin{equation}
\mathit{slot\_adjustment} \gets \mathit{slot\_adjustment} + S_{\mathrm{adj}}
\end{equation}
\begin{equation}
\mathit{us\_adjustment} \gets \mathit{us\_adjustment} - \mu s_{\mathrm{adj}}
\end{equation}
The negative sign in the microsecond adjustment reduces the local thread sleep time when the VNF clock is behind, speeding up the slot transition rate to catch up. The synchronization process is summarized in Algorithm~\ref{alg:node_sync_simplified}.

\begin{algorithm}[H]
\caption{Node sync}
\label{alg:node_sync_simplified}
\small
\begin{algorithmic}[1]
\algrenewcommand\algorithmicrequire{\textbf{Input:}}
\algrenewcommand\algorithmicensure{\textbf{Output:}}
\Require $t_1, t_2, t_3$ (Timestamps from PNF \textit{UL\_node\_sync}), $t_4$ (VNF local receive timestamp)
\Ensure $\mathit{slot\_adjustment}$ (Slot adjustment accumulator), $\mathit{us\_adjustment}$ (Microsecond adjustment accumulator)

\Statex \textbf{Notation:} 
\Statex $\mathit{offset}$ (Instantaneous timing offset), $\mathit{sync\_locked}$ (Synchronization lock state)
\Statex $T_{\mathrm{slot}}$ (Slot duration in \textmu s), $T_{\mathrm{wrap}}$ (Wrap-around period, $10240000$ \textmu s)
\Statex $\text{WrapCorrection}(x, T)$ (Corrects $x$ within $[-T/2, T/2]$)

\Statex \textbf{Phase I: Timing Offset Calculation}
\State $d_1 \gets \text{WrapCorrection}(t_2 - t_1, T_{\mathrm{wrap}})$
\State $d_2 \gets \text{WrapCorrection}(t_4 - t_3, T_{\mathrm{wrap}})$
\State $\mathit{offset} \gets (d_1 - d_2) / 2$

\Statex \textbf{Phase II: Hysteresis Lock and Clock Adjustment}
\If{$\mathit{sync\_locked} = 1 \land |\mathit{offset}| > T_{\mathrm{slot}}$}
    \State $\mathit{sync\_locked} \gets 0$ \Comment{Unlock due to excessive clock drift}
\EndIf

\If{$\mathit{sync\_locked} = 0$}
    \If{$|\mathit{offset}| \le T_{\mathrm{slot}}$}
        \State $\mathit{sync\_locked} \gets 1$ \Comment{Lock synchronization}
    \Else
        \State $S_{\mathrm{adj}} \gets \mathit{offset} / T_{\mathrm{slot}}$ \Comment{Integer division}
        \State $\mu s_{\mathrm{adj}} \gets \mathit{offset} \pmod{T_{\mathrm{slot}}}$
        \State $\mathit{slot\_adjustment} \gets \mathit{slot\_adjustment} + S_{\mathrm{adj}}$
        \State $\mathit{us\_adjustment} \gets \mathit{us\_adjustment} - \mu s_{\mathrm{adj}}$
    \EndIf
\EndIf
\State \Return $\mathit{slot\_adjustment}, \mathit{us\_adjustment}$
\end{algorithmic}
\end{algorithm}

\section{Delay Management}
\label{subsec:delay_management}

To counteract changing network latency and maintain scheduling stability, a dynamic delay management strategy is implemented. This strategy operates as a closed-loop feedback mechanism based on standard Small Cell Forum (SCF) PNF timing info reported by the PNF. By evaluating the arrival margin relative to the PNF deadline, the algorithm dynamically adjusts the scheduling lead time $S_{\mathrm{ahead}}$.

\subsection{Timing Model}
\label{subsec:timing_model}
To formalize the delay management problem, this section introduces the mathematical timing model of the nFAPI-based Split Option 6 architecture. Let $T_{\mathrm{slot}}$ denote the slot duration and $S_{\mathrm{ahead}}$ denote the number of slots by which the VNF scheduler triggers earlier than the target slot. For a scheduling message generated at VNF time $t_g$, the corresponding target PNF processing deadline is determined by the target slot boundary. The arrival margin reported by the PNF is denoted by $\Delta t_{\mathrm{arrive}}$, where $\Delta t_{\mathrm{arrive}} < 0$ indicates that the message arrives before the deadline (early arrival), and $\Delta t_{\mathrm{arrive}} > 0$ indicates a late arrival. Therefore, the delay management objective is to select the minimum stable $S_{\mathrm{ahead}}$ such that late arrivals are avoided ($\Delta t_{\mathrm{arrive}} \le 0$) while the MAC HARQ Round-Trip Time (RTT) is not unnecessarily inflated.


\subsection{EWMA-based Arrival Margin Estimation}
\label{subsubsec:ewma_estimation}
To smooth high-frequency network noise while remaining sensitive to timing drift, the algorithm maintains a running mean of the PNF timing info ($\mu_{\mathrm{arrive}}$) and the estimated jitter ($dev_{\mathrm{arrive}}$). Let $\Delta t_{\mathrm{arrive}, i}$ be the observed signed PNF timing info in scheduling slot $i$. The EWMA updates for mean arrival margin and jitter are defined as:
\begin{equation}
\mu_{\mathrm{arrive}, i} = (1 - \alpha) \mu_{\mathrm{arrive}, i-1} + \alpha \Delta t_{\mathrm{arrive}, i}
\end{equation}
\begin{equation}
dev_{\mathrm{arrive}, i} = (1 - \beta) dev_{\mathrm{arrive}, i-1} + \beta |\Delta t_{\mathrm{arrive}, i} - \mu_{\mathrm{arrive}, i}|
\end{equation}
where the smoothing factors are chosen as $\alpha = 1/8$ to track the long-term trend, and $\beta = 1/4$ to capture fast jitter variations. These parameters are designed as negative powers of two ($\alpha = 2^{-3}$, $\beta = 2^{-2}$) to allow the algorithm to perform fast bitwise shift operations in real time.

The combined timing risk indicator $R_i$ is evaluated to determine whether the arrival margin crosses the deadline boundary:
\begin{equation}
R_i = \mu_{\mathrm{arrive}, i} + dev_{\mathrm{arrive}, i}
\end{equation}
A positive timing risk ($R_i > 0$) implies that the estimated worst-case arrival margin crosses the deadline, indicating an anomaly condition, whereas a negative risk indicates a safe margin.

\subsection{Adaptive Slots Ahead Control}
\label{subsubsec:delay_control}
The update of the slots ahead trigger timing $S_{\mathrm{ahead}}$ is governed by a fast-increase and conservative-decrease control law to optimize latency while preserving throughput under dynamic congestion:
\begin{itemize}
    \item \textbf{Rapid Increase (Preserve Throughput):} If an anomaly timing risk is detected ($R_i > 0$), indicating that the scheduling message is crossing the PNF processing deadline, the algorithm immediately increases the slots ahead value to prevent late packet arrivals and missing-slot dropouts:
          \begin{equation}
          S_{\mathrm{ahead}, i+1} = \min\left(S_{\mathrm{ahead}, i} + \left\lceil \frac{R_i}{T_{\mathrm{slot}}} \right\rceil, S_{\max}\right)
          \end{equation}
          and the stable observation counter $C_{\mathrm{stable}}$ is reset to 0. Here, $S_{\max} = \lfloor T_{\mathrm{window}} / T_{\mathrm{slot}} \rfloor$ represents the maximum allowed slots ahead.
    \item \textbf{Gradual Decrease (Optimize Latency):} If the system is under stable and safe conditions ($R_i < -dev_{\mathrm{arrive}, i}$), indicating that there is a sufficient timing margin, the algorithm decrements the slots ahead value by one slot once the stability condition is satisfied ($C_{\mathrm{stable}} \ge C_{\mathrm{threshold}}$):
          \begin{equation}
          S_{\mathrm{ahead}, i+1} = \max\left(S_{\mathrm{ahead}, i} - 1, S_{\min}\right)
          \end{equation}
          and resets $C_{\mathrm{stable}} = 0$ to prevent premature timing reductions. Here, $S_{\min} = 1$ is the minimum slots ahead trigger offset.
    \item \textbf{Maintain Baseline:} Otherwise, the trigger timing remains unchanged:
          \begin{equation}
          S_{\mathrm{ahead}, i+1} = S_{\mathrm{ahead}, i}
          \end{equation}
          and the stable counter $C_{\mathrm{stable}}$ is incremented if the current sample is safe ($R_i \le 0$).
\end{itemize}

The core delay management strategy is condensed in Algorithm~\ref{alg:delay_management}.

\begin{algorithm}[!htbp]
\caption{Delay Management}
\label{alg:delay_management}
\footnotesize
\begin{algorithmic}[1]
\algrenewcommand\algorithmicrequire{\textbf{Input:}}
\algrenewcommand\algorithmicensure{\textbf{Output:}}
\Require $\Delta t_{\mathrm{arrive}}$ (Observed PNF Timing Info), $S_{\mathrm{curr}}$ (Current trigger timing), $T_{\mathrm{window}}$ (Timing window), $T_{\mathrm{slot}}$ (Slot duration)
\Ensure $S_{\mathrm{next}}$ (Trigger timing for the next MAC schedule)
\Statex 
\Statex \textbf{Notation:} 
\Statex $\mu_{\mathrm{arrive}}$ (smoothed mean PNF Timing Info), $dev_{\mathrm{arrive}}$ (smoothed arrival jitter)
\Statex $R$ (Timing risk indicator), $C_{\mathrm{stable}}$ (Stable observation counter)
\Statex $C_{\mathrm{threshold}}$ (Stability observation threshold), $S_{\max}, S_{\min}$ (Boundary limits)
\Statex $\alpha, \beta$ (Smoothing factors for latency and jitter)
 
\Statex \textbf{Phase I: Latency and Jitter Estimation}
\State $\mu_{\mathrm{arrive}} \gets (1 - \alpha)\mu_{\mathrm{arrive}} + \alpha \Delta t_{\mathrm{arrive}}$ \Comment{Update mean PNF Timing Info}
\State $dev_{\mathrm{arrive}} \gets (1 - \beta)dev_{\mathrm{arrive}} + \beta |\Delta t_{\mathrm{arrive}} - \mu_{\mathrm{arrive}}|$ \Comment{Update arrival jitter}
\State $R \gets \mu_{\mathrm{arrive}} + dev_{\mathrm{arrive}}$ \Comment{Evaluate combined risk indicator}

\Statex \textbf{Phase II: Fast-Up and Slow-Down Timing Adjustment}
\If{$R > 0$}
    \State $S_{\mathrm{next}} \gets \min(S_{\mathrm{curr}} + \lceil R / T_{\mathrm{slot}} \rceil, S_{\max})$ \Comment{Rapid Increase}
    \State $C_{\mathrm{stable}} \gets 0$
\ElsIf{$R < -dev_{\mathrm{arrive}}$}
    \If{$C_{\mathrm{stable}} \ge C_{\mathrm{threshold}}$}
        \State $S_{\mathrm{next}} \gets \max(S_{\mathrm{curr}} - 1, S_{\min})$ \Comment{Gradual Decrease}
        \State $C_{\mathrm{stable}} \gets 0$
    \Else
        \State $S_{\mathrm{next}} \gets S_{\mathrm{curr}}$
        \State $C_{\mathrm{stable}} \gets C_{\mathrm{stable}} + 1$
    \EndIf
\Else
    \State $S_{\mathrm{next}} \gets S_{\mathrm{curr}}$
    \If{$R \le 0$}
        \State $C_{\mathrm{stable}} \gets C_{\mathrm{stable}} + 1$
    \Else
        \State $C_{\mathrm{stable}} \gets 0$
    \EndIf
\EndIf
\State \Return $S_{\mathrm{next}}$
\end{algorithmic}
\end{algorithm}

\subsection{Coordination between Node sync and Delay Management}
\label{subsec:coordination}

To ensure stable operation under real-world conditions, Node sync and Delay Management must coordinate without inducing control loop oscillations. The microsecond-level Node sync focuses strictly on correcting clock phase offsets to align the VNF's scheduling thread with the PNF's slot transitions. Because it directly shifts the VNF's time base, any clock adjustment ($S_{adj}, \mu s_{adj}$) temporarily shifts the arrival timestamp reference. Delay Management is designed to absorb this transient shifting. By relying on history-weighted EWMA-based risk tracking ($R_i$), Delay Management avoids reacting to the temporary timing offsets caused by clock adjustments, allowing Node sync to converge. Once Node sync locks synchronization ($\mathit{sync\_locked} = 1$), the time base stabilizes, allowing Delay Management to achieve precise slot-level optimizations ($S_{\mathrm{ahead}}$).
